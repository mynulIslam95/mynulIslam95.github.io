The address on the shelf

The paper is not the money. Two workers, one shelf. This is the hinge for OS.

Story

The box money sits on a shelf. The shelf has a number. That number is the address.

You write the address on a paper. The paper is not the money. The paper only says where the box lives.

Worker A and Worker B both hold a copy of that paper. They walk to the same shelf. If A puts 50 in the box, B sees 50.

In C the paper is a pointer. &money means “the address of this box.” *p means “open the box at this address.”

You will need this for a mutex, a semaphore, and mmap. Without the paper, those words are magic.

Practice. If I copy the paper, do I get a second box of money?

No. You copy the address, not the money. Two papers, one box.

Practice. If two workers have the same paper, and one writes 50, does the other see 50?

Yes. Same shelf. That is why two threads can smash the same x. That is also why a lock can sit at one address both can open.

The same story in C

#include <stdio.h>

int main(void) {
  int money = 10;
  int *p = &money;
  *p = 50;
  printf("money=%d\n", money);
  return 0;
}

money is the box. p is the paper. &money fills the paper. *p = 50 opens the box and writes 50. printf shows 50.

Practice. After *p = 50, is money 50?

Yes. You did not make a second box. You opened the same box through the paper.

Practice. Is p the money?

No. p is the address. *p is the money inside.
