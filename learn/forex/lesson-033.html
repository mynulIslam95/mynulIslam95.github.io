Doji, Hammer, Shooting Star and Engulfing Patterns

Learn precise candlestick pattern definitions, distinguish shape from market context and test pattern hypotheses without assuming guaranteed reversals.

Doji, Hammer, Shooting Star ও Engulfing Pattern-এর Formula, Context, Example, Risk ও Scientific Backtesting শিখবে। Pattern মানেই নিশ্চিত Reversal নয়।

Learning outcomes
  1. Identify four classic pattern families using explicit, reproducible OHLC rules.
  2. Distinguish a hammer from a hanging man and a shooting star from an inverted hammer using prior trend context.
  3. Calculate body, wick and range ratios in pips with zero-range safeguards.
  4. Recognize conflicting textbook definitions, intrabar ambiguity and execution costs.
  5. Design and critically evaluate a realistic candle-pattern study.

1. Candlestick anatomy and pattern illustrations

2. Doji: small body, not a prediction

A strict doji has O=C. In practical chart scanners, a near-doji often uses a body/range threshold. This lesson uses a declared teaching threshold of body/range ≤ 0.10 for nonzero-range bars. It is an illustrative definition, not a universal industry standard.

Worked EUR/USD example

O=1.0850, H=1.0870, L=1.0830, C=1.0852. Body 2 pips; range 40 pips; body ratio 2/40=5%. The bar meets our near-doji rule but is not a strict doji because O≠C.

Body খুব ছোট মানেই পরের Candle উল্টো দিকে যাবে না। Strict Doji-তে Open ও Close সমান।

3. Hammer and hanging man

A hammer-shaped candle has a small body near the high and a relatively long lower wick. For a reproducible exercise, define: range > 0, body/range ≤ 0.35, lower wick ≥ 2 × body and upper wick/range ≤ 0.15. If body=0, additionally require lower wick/range ≥ 0.50 so a zero-body candle is not accepted merely because lower ≥ 0. The shape becomes a hammer reversal candidate only with an independently defined prior downtrend. The same shape after an uptrend is often called a hanging man.

Worked hammer-shaped example

EUR/USD O=1.0845, H=1.0847, L=1.0810, C=1.0840. Body 5 pips, lower wick 30 pips, upper wick 2 pips, range 37 pips. Body/range ≈13.51%, lower/body=6 and upper/range≈5.41%. Shape qualifies under this lesson's rules. Prior trend has not been supplied, so a reversal interpretation is not established.

Hammer Shape থাকলেও আগের Downtrend না জানলে Hammer Reversal Signal বলা ঠিক নয়।

4. Shooting star and inverted hammer

A shooting-star shape places a small body near the low and a long upper wick. Here the illustrative rule is: range > 0, body/range ≤ 0.35, upper wick ≥ 2 × body and lower wick/range ≤ 0.15. For zero-body candles additionally require upper wick/range ≥ 0.50. The name shooting star normally implies prior uptrend context. In a prior downtrend, the same shape may be interpreted as an inverted hammer.

Worked shooting-star-shaped example

EUR/USD O=1.0820, H=1.0860, L=1.0818, C=1.0825. Body 5 pips, upper wick 35 pips, lower wick 2 pips, range 42 pips. Body/range≈11.90%; upper/body=7; lower/range≈4.76%. Shape qualifies, but the prior trend and subsequent result are unknown.

Upper Wick বড় মানেই Bank Sell করেছে, এমন প্রমাণ নেই। Pattern-এর Context আলাদা করে যাচাই করো।

5. Bullish and bearish engulfing

Engulfing patterns use two completed candles. Under this lesson's real-body definition, bullish engulfing requires candle 1 bearish (C₁<O₁), candle 2 bullish (C₂>O₂), and the second real body contains the first: O₂ ≤ C₁ and C₂ ≥ O₁. At least one containment boundary must be strict. Bearish engulfing reverses directions: candle 1 bullish, candle 2 bearish, O₂ ≥ C₁ and C₂ ≤ O₁, with at least one strict boundary. Some sources require strict inequalities, gaps or entire-range containment; document your choice before testing.

Worked bullish engulfing

First EUR/USD candle: O₁=1.0860, C₁=1.0840 (bearish, 20-pip body). Second: O₂=1.0835, C₂=1.0865 (bullish, 30-pip body). Because 1.0835 ≤ 1.0840 and 1.0865 ≥ 1.0860, the second real body engulfs the first. Both candles must also have valid highs and lows.

Engulfing Pattern-এর Body আর Full High-Low Range এক জিনিস নয়। কোন Definition ব্যবহার করছো আগে লিখবে।

7. Institutional interpretation and common myths

ObservationWhat it supportsWhat it cannot establish
DojiClose near open relative to bar rangeBuyers and sellers are equally numerous
Hammer shapePrice low is far below bodyInstitutional accumulation occurred
Shooting-star shapePrice high is far above bodyStop hunting or guaranteed reversal
Engulfing real bodiesTwo-candle open/close containmentGuaranteed next-bar momentum

Every executed transaction has both a buyer and a seller. OHLC cannot reveal the full order-flow tape, order intent, venue access or the sequence of high and low. Dealer quote feeds and exchange-traded futures candles are not interchangeable.

8. Scientific backtesting protocol

  1. Predefine pattern thresholds, prior-trend definition and the next-bar outcome. Record all tested alternatives.
  2. Use a timestamped, auditable FX dataset with timezone, source, bid/ask conventions and missing-data handling.
  3. Calculate a signal only when the final candle of the pattern has closed. Never use future bars to classify the prior trend.
  4. Enter at the next executable bid/ask as appropriate. Include variable spreads, commission, slippage, financing, latency and gaps.
  5. Compare with an unconditional baseline and a no-trade benchmark. Distinguish statistical from economic significance.
  6. Use chronological development and untouched holdout data. Report number of signals, confidence intervals, net expectancy, drawdown and regime sensitivity.
  7. Stress-test parameter choices, quote feeds and execution costs. Avoid treating multiple tested thresholds as independent confirmations.
  8. Publish negative results and limitations. A backtest does not guarantee future profits.

Backtest-এ Future Candle-এর Data আগে ব্যবহার করবে না। Spread, Slippage, Commission, Holdout Data ও Baseline লাগবে।

  1. Generate 200 deterministic synthetic valid OHLC candles and label strict doji, near-doji, hammer-shaped and shooting-star-shaped bars.

  5. Design a preregistered research hypothesis with executable costs and a chronological holdout.

10. Mastery examination · 20 questions

Answer each question before expanding the solution.

Q1. Is every small-body candle a doji?

No. Define a numerical body/range threshold and distinguish a strict O=C doji from near-doji conventions.

Q2. Does a doji guarantee reversal?

No. It describes open-close proximity, not a forecast.

Q3. What is a hammer's defining geometry?

A small real body near the upper end of the bar with a comparatively long lower shadow, under a stated threshold.

Q4. Does a hammer require a preceding downtrend?

For a reversal-pattern interpretation, yes. Without context it is simply hammer-shaped.

Q5. What is a hanging man?

A hammer-shaped candle after an uptrend; the context and interpretation differ.

Q6. What characterizes a shooting star?

Small body near the low with a comparatively long upper wick, usually interpreted in an uptrend context.

Q7. What is an inverted hammer?

A shooting-star-shaped candle after a downtrend, interpreted differently by context.

Q8. What is a bullish engulfing pattern?

Under a common real-body definition, a bearish candle followed by a bullish candle whose real body contains the first candle's body.

Q9. Does engulfing require the second candle's full range to engulf the first?

Not under the real-body definition. Other definitions exist and must be specified.

Q10. Does the engulfing pattern require a price gap in FX?

Not necessarily under a body-containment rule; FX gaps and feed conventions complicate textbook definitions.

Q11. Can a candle meet multiple pattern labels?

Yes, depending on definitions and contextual filters.

Q12. Why should pattern thresholds be specified before testing?

To reduce cherry-picking and multiple-testing bias.

Q13. What does a long upper wick prove?

Only that the bar's high exceeded its body top; not that institutions sold.

Q14. Can identical OHLC bars have different tick paths?

Yes. OHLC does not preserve the order of intrabar extrema.

Q15. Why can a midpoint backtest exaggerate a strategy's performance?

It ignores executable bid/ask spread and potentially slippage and commission.

Q16. When can a signal be used without look-ahead?

Only after the full pattern's last required candle has closed.

Q17. What is the difference between reversal and continuation?

A reversal changes a defined prior direction; continuation persists in it. Pattern shape alone establishes neither.

Q18. Why compare against a baseline?

A raw win rate or return may not exceed what a simple no-signal or unconditional rule achieves.

Q19. What should an out-of-sample test report?

Net returns, sample size, uncertainty, drawdown, sensitivity to costs and tested variations.

Q20. Does a positive backtest prove future profitability?

No. Regime changes, costs, overfitting and execution differences can eliminate apparent edges.

Independent professional mastery

Define and defend your own fixed pattern rules, then test them on synthetic data and, if available, responsibly sourced real historical bid/ask observations. Compare pattern frequency and net returns with a predeclared baseline. Report all unsuccessful hypotheses.

নিজের Rule আগে লিখবে, পরে Data Test করবে। Result পছন্দ না হলে Rule বদলে আবার Test করার Bias ব্যাখ্যা করবে।

Fully worked independent case

EUR/USD O=1.0845, H=1.0847, L=1.0810, C=1.0840. Body 5 pips, upper 2, lower 30 and range 37. Ratio tests pass our hammer-shaped rule. However, without a prior-trend definition and executable trading-cost data, this is not a verified reversal signal.

Change the close to 1.0812. Does it still qualify?

No. Body = 33 pips and range = 37 pips, so body/range ≈ 89.19%, exceeding the 35% threshold. It is bearish, but not hammer-shaped under this rule.

Institutional evidence and further study

Pattern terminology is not universally standardized. These numeric definitions are transparent teaching rules rather than claims of validated predictive power. For FX market conduct, execution and OTC market structure, consult:

  • Global Foreign Exchange Committee: FX Global Code
  • BIS: OTC FX turnover survey methodology
  • CME Group: FX futures data and contract context

These references provide market background, not evidence that any candle pattern is profitable.

Risk and limitations

Leveraged Forex and CFDs can lead to rapid losses. Pattern classification does not establish a trading edge. Spreads, gaps, liquidity, financing and counterparty terms affect actual outcomes. Use paper or simulated trading for coursework.
