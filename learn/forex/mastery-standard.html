Professional FX research and trading education

Forex Mastery Standard

Our objective is deep, evidence-based expertise in foreign exchange, not memorizing chart patterns or chasing a fixed return. This is a curriculum standard and study guide, not an accredited qualification or evidence of trading proficiency.

Required competency pillars

  1. FX mechanics: Spot, forwards, swaps, futures, options, CFDs, quotes, settlement, margin and execution.
  2. Macroeconomic FX: Rates, central banks, inflation, capital flows, external balances, cross-currency basis and market regimes.
  3. Market microstructure: OTC fragmentation, dealer inventory, bid/ask dynamics, liquidity, venue differences and execution cost.
  4. Research methods: Probability, statistical inference, data cleaning, reproducibility, hypothesis testing, multiple-testing bias and walk-forward evaluation.
  5. Strategy design: Written rules, explicit assumptions, benchmark comparisons, realistic transaction costs and failure conditions.
  6. Risk and operations: Drawdown, leverage, correlated currency exposure, stress testing, counterparty risk, security and regulatory due diligence.
  7. Professional judgment: Distinguish observation from speculation. Treat ICT and Smart Money Concepts as testable claims, not established institutional facts.

Professional research progression

Stage A · Foundation and market literacy

Complete lessons 001–030, build quote and execution calculators and pass oral or written explanations of core concepts. No live trading required.

Stage B · Analytical competence

Work through the remaining price action, liquidity, macroeconomic and risk modules. Compare competing explanations against observable data. Do not infer hidden institutional intent from a chart alone.

Stage C · Quantitative validation

Build at least three clearly specified FX research hypotheses. For each, record data provenance, train/test splits, out-of-sample results, spreads, commissions, slippage scenarios, robustness checks and a simple benchmark. Negative findings count as useful research.

Stage D · Controlled execution

Practice using demo orders and an execution journal across different volatility regimes. Record rejected orders, missed fills, adherence to rules, exposure, drawdown and operational failures.

Stage E · Professional review

Prepare a research dossier that another person can reproduce. Explain limitations and demonstrate that results are not dependent on a single hand-picked period. There is no guaranteed passing profit target.

Critical institutional topics to deepen

  • Covered interest parity, forward points and FX swap pricing
  • Cross-currency basis, hedging demand and dollar funding
  • Central bank reaction functions and rate-differential scenarios
  • Dealer inventory, adverse selection, market impact and last-look practices
  • Portfolio currency exposure, stress scenarios and liquidity shocks
  • Data leakage, survivorship bias, overfitting and multiple-hypothesis testing
  • Out-of-sample evaluation, Monte Carlo analysis and risk of ruin
  • Counterparty, custody, broker and operational security risks

Reference starting points

  • BIS: Foreign exchange turnover survey
  • FX Global Code
  • Bank for International Settlements research
  • CME Group FX and derivatives education
  • CFTC investor education

Risk and expectations: A goal of turning $1,000 into $100,000 in a year requires a 9,900% return and roughly 46.8% compounded monthly growth. This is an extreme and unreliable financial target. Do not use it as a proficiency benchmark. Leverage can produce losses beyond intended risk limits depending on the product and jurisdiction.
