Pin Bars and Rejection Candles

Quantify long-wick candles, interpret their limitations and learn how to test price rejection without mistaking candle shapes for proof of institutional intent.

Learning outcomes
  1. Calculate body, upper wick, lower wick and total range in pips.
  2. Apply explicit bullish and bearish pin-bar classification rules.
  3. Separate a candle's shape from trend, location and subsequent confirmation.
  4. Recognize price-feed differences, intrabar uncertainty and realistic execution costs.
  5. Build and evaluate a reproducible pin-bar hypothesis with negative controls.

2. Precise anatomy and operational definitions

Body = |Close − Open|
Upper = High − max(Open, Close)
Lower = min(Open, Close) − Low
Range = High − Low

OHLC must satisfy H≥max(O,C) and L≤min(O,C). For EUR/USD, 1 pip = 0.0001; for USD/JPY, 1 pip = 0.01. If range=0, all range ratios are undefined and this lesson labels the candle neither.

Illustrative teaching rules, not universal definitions: body/range ≤ 0.30; the dominant wick/range ≥ 0.60; the opposite wick/range ≤ 0.15. A candle is bullish pin-shaped when the lower wick dominates and bearish pin-shaped when the upper wick dominates. These mutually exclusive thresholds intentionally avoid ambiguous dual labels.

Bullish: B/R ≤ 0.30, Lower/R ≥ 0.60, Upper/R ≤ 0.15
Bearish: B/R ≤ 0.30, Upper/R ≥ 0.60, Lower/R ≤ 0.15

3. Fully worked Forex examples

A. Bullish pin-shaped EUR/USD

O=1.0840, H=1.0843, L=1.0800, C=1.0842. Body=2 pips, upper=1 pip, lower=40 pips and range=43 pips. Body/range=4.65%, lower/range=93.02%, upper/range=2.33%. It qualifies as bullish pin-shaped. No preceding trend or subsequent outcome is provided.

B. Bearish pin-shaped USD/JPY

O=150.20, H=150.85, L=150.17, C=150.18. Body=2 pips, upper=65 pips, lower=1 pip and range=68 pips. Body/range≈2.94%, upper/range≈95.59%, lower/range≈1.47%. It qualifies as bearish pin-shaped. USD/JPY pip size is 0.01, not 0.0001.

C. Large body, not a pin bar

EUR/USD O=1.0800, H=1.0850, L=1.0790, C=1.0840. Body=40 pips and range=60 pips, so body/range=66.67%. It fails the small-body rule even though the lower wick is visible.

Pin bar and rejection candle are descriptive trading terms with inconsistent definitions across sources. A candle's name does not identify an institutional participant, a market maker's inventory or a stop-hunting event.

6. Context: where a wick occurs matters

To test whether location adds information, define reference levels before observing the signal: for example a previous-day high or low, a rolling N-bar extreme using completed bars only or a documented session boundary. Compare the pin-shaped subset near a predefined level with the same shape away from that level. Avoid drawing support or resistance retrospectively around winning examples.

A bullish pin-shaped candle after a decline and a bearish pin-shaped candle during an uptrend can be interpreted as reversal candidates. The same shapes may also occur in a continuation or a random volatility episode. The conditional outcomes must be measured, not assumed.

7. What rejection candles cannot prove

A long wick establishes that the candle traded to an extreme and closed closer to the opposite side. It does not show whether the high occurred before the low, which participant caused the move, whether stops were triggered or whether institutional accumulation took place. Two different tick sequences can produce the same OHLC.

FX is decentralized and feed-dependent. Bid, ask and midpoint candles can have different extrema. A midpoint high above a threshold does not establish that a buy-stop order was executed at that midpoint. Actual fills depend on ask/bid quotes, order type, liquidity, latency and slippage.

8. Trading hypotheses, costs and statistical testing

  1. Pre-register pair, timeframe, quote source, session, pin-bar thresholds and any trend/location filters.
  2. Compute signals only after the candle closes. If confirmation requires a later candle, delay the decision accordingly.
  3. Choose one clearly specified entry rule, exit rule, maximum holding period and risk limit before seeing outcomes.
  4. Use executable bid/ask prices, variable spread, commission, financing, gaps, latency and conservative slippage assumptions.
  5. Compare against unconditional baseline trades, a no-signal control and matched volatility periods.
  6. Use chronological development and untouched holdout samples. Report sample size, net expectancy, uncertainty intervals, drawdown and loss tails.
  7. Stress-test alternative thresholds and data feeds, accounting for multiple comparisons. Publish negative findings.
  8. Never infer the order of stop and target hits from a single OHLC bar without finer-grained data.

Pattern পাওয়া আর Profitable Strategy পাওয়া এক নয়। Spread, Commission, Holdout এবং Baseline ছাড়া দাবি করা যাবে না।

10. Mastery examination · 20 questions

Q1. What is a pin bar?

A candle with a small body near one end and a relatively long opposite wick, under a stated numerical rule.

Q2. Is pin bar a universally standardized term?

No. Thresholds vary across educators and platforms.

Q3. What does rejection mean in candle terminology?

Price traded into a region and closed away from an extreme; it does not prove who traded or why.

Q4. What is a bullish pin-bar shape?

A long lower wick, comparatively small body and limited upper wick.

Q5. What is a bearish pin-bar shape?

A long upper wick, comparatively small body and limited lower wick.

Q6. Can a doji be a pin bar?

Under some definitions yes, but this lesson requires an extreme-side wick to dominate the range.

Q7. What is the body/range ratio?

Absolute close minus open divided by high minus low, provided range is positive.

Q8. What is a bullish rejection ratio?

Lower wick divided by total range, in this lesson.

Q9. What is a bearish rejection ratio?

Upper wick divided by total range, in this lesson.

Q10. Does a long lower wick prove institutional buying?

No. OHLC does not identify trader type or order intent.

Q11. How does a hammer differ from a pin bar?

Their shapes may overlap; hammer is usually tied to downtrend reversal context, whereas pin bar is often a broader descriptive label.

Q12. Why does location matter?

A shape at a predefined reference level may have a different conditional outcome from the same shape elsewhere, but that must be tested.

Q13. Does support guarantee a bounce?

No. Support is an analytical reference, not a guaranteed executable price response.

Q14. What is confirmation?

A separately defined later condition, such as a subsequent close above the pin-bar high; it introduces delay and must not leak future data.

Q15. Can one OHLC candle reveal high-low sequence?

No. Multiple intrabar paths can have identical OHLC.

Q16. How do spreads affect a pin-bar breakout?

Ask/bid prices and spread determine whether a buy or sell order actually triggers and fills.

Q17. What is a false positive?

A candle meeting shape criteria without the hypothesized favorable subsequent outcome.

Q18. Why predefine thresholds?

To avoid tuning the definition after observing outcomes.

Q19. What should a holdout evaluation include?

Signal count, net results, uncertainty, costs, drawdown and a benchmark.

Q20. Does a backtest prove future profit?

No. It may fail due to changing regimes, execution differences and overfitting.

Independent calculation and counterexample

EUR/USD O=1.0840, H=1.0843, L=1.0800, C=1.0842. Lower wick=40 pips and range=43 pips. Under the 60% lower-wick threshold, the bar qualifies. Change the low to 1.0838 while leaving other prices unchanged: lower wick=2 pips and range=5 pips, so lower/range=40%. The candle no longer qualifies. This illustrates how a small change in observed extrema can change a label.

Risk and limitations

Leveraged Forex and CFDs carry substantial loss risk. A pin bar is a price-shape observation, not a guaranteed signal or an explanation of who traded. Use simulations for practice and do not treat synthetic examples as market evidence.
